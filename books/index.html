SwiftUI for KMP Developers

A Side-by-Side Guide to SwiftUI for Android Developers Who Know Jetpack Compose

You already know most of SwiftUI. Your team adopted Kotlin Multiplatform, and half of your app's future moved into Xcode, in a language that looks almost like Kotlin, full of @State, $bindings and some View.

This book teaches SwiftUI the fastest way an Android developer can learn it: from Compose. Every topic starts with the Compose code you already write, shows the SwiftUI code that does the same job side by side, and explains exactly where the two differ.

Column→VStack remember→@State LaunchedEffect→.task CompositionLocal→@Environment
What's inside
  • Swift and Xcode, explained through Kotlin and Android Studio
  • Layout, modifiers, lists and navigation, side by side with Compose
  • Modern SwiftUI state with @Observable, bindings and the environment
  • How Kotlin code looks from Swift, and using Flows and suspend functions with SKIE
  • Driving one shared Kotlin ViewModel from both Compose and SwiftUI
  • Dependency injection with Koin across Android and iOS
  • Testing, accessibility, performance and iOS design conventions
  • A complete app, Jotter, built twice: once in Compose, once in SwiftUI
  • A cheat sheet after every chapter, plus a full Compose-to-SwiftUI lookup table and a Kotlin-to-Swift syntax guide

Who it's for: Android developers who know Kotlin and Jetpack Compose and need to build or understand native SwiftUI screens in a Kotlin Multiplatform app.

Questions about the books

Where can I buy the books?

All five books are published on Amazon through Kindle Direct Publishing. Click "Buy on Amazon" on any book to see the available formats and price in your country.

I'm a junior developer. Are the interview books too advanced?

No. Every interview book is written to take you from junior to senior, so it starts with the fundamentals and builds up to the questions asked in senior interviews.

Which book should I read first?

Start with Kotlin if the language itself still feels shaky, or Core Android if you're preparing for your first Android role. Then move to Coroutines & Flow and Jetpack Compose, which come up in almost every modern Android interview. Working on a KMP app with a native iOS UI? Read SwiftUI for KMP Developers.

How are the books different from the courses?

The books focus on interview questions and clear explanations. The courses are hands-on: you build complete apps from scratch. Many people use the books to prepare for interviews and the courses to level up their day-to-day skills.
